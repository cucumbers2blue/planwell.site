Grade 7 Music — Task A: History of Music Notation

Submission tag: `taskA`

Big idea: Notation systems change when musicians need to communicate in new ways.

Your Task

Complete both strands of Criterion A:

1. Strand i — Investigate: Answer all guided questions in The Pantry.

2. Strand ii — Analyse: Annotate one historical music manuscript.

Strand i — Guided Questions

Use full sentences and details from the videos.

Questions

1. What were neumes? What did they help singers do?

2. Why could early neumes not show an exact melody? What did singers need to know?

3. How did heightened neumes give musicians more information?

4. How did adding a line make pitch clearer?

5. Who is linked to the four-line staff? What could it show and not show?

6. What problem did rhythmic modes try to solve?

7. How did changing note shapes help musicians read rhythm?

8. What did the Ars Nova movement improve?

9. Name three features added to modern notation. What does each tell a musician?

10. Choose one change in notation. What musical problem did it solve?

Explain how and why notation changed, including ideas such as oral tradition, neumes, staff lines, rhythm and printing.

Strand ii — Manuscript Annotation

Find one historical manuscript and show it to your teacher before you begin.

Add labels that explain:

  • the symbols and musical information you can see
  • how the notation works
  • when or why it was used
  • what it communicates clearly
  • what it leaves unclear or expects the performer to know

You may annotate digitally or on paper. Clear explanations matter more than the tool.

Quick Examples

Use these as models. Only write about features visible in your manuscript.

Strand i — Why Notation Changed

Example 1: Pitch

Weak: “Staff lines made music better.”

Strong: “Staff lines showed pitch more accurately. Singers could learn unfamiliar melodies with less help.”

Example 2: Printing

Weak: “People started printing music.”

Strong: “Hand-copying was slow and could create mistakes. Printing helped musicians share matching copies more widely.”

Strand ii — Analysing a Manuscript

Example 1: Clef

Weak: “This symbol is a clef.”

Strong: “The C clef shows where C is on the staff. This helps the singer find the other pitches.”

Example 2: Text and Rhythm

Weak: “There are words under the notes.”

Strong: “The words line up with the notes, showing which syllable to sing. The rhythm is unclear, so the singer needed to know the style.”
