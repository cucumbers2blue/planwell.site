Grade 8 Music — Task A: Percussion

Criterion: A — Investigating

Submission tag: `taskA`

The Task

You will investigate:

  • how percussion developed in different cultures
  • how percussion has been used for music, communication and ceremony
  • how modern notation helps musicians perform percussion music

You must complete both strands.

Strand i — Guided Questions

Watch:

History of Percussion Instruments: 5000 Years of Percussion

Answer every question below in full sentences. Use information from the video and your knowledge of modern drum notation.

#### Percussion History

1. Before formal instruments existed, how did early humans create rhythm? Give two examples. (0:11–0:15)

2. What materials did early humans combine to make the first true drums? (1:26–1:33)

3. How can the African talking drum imitate features of human speech? What was it used for? (2:51–2:57)

4. When did the tabla emerge? Describe its importance in Indian classical music. (5:24–5:26)

5. What were the Bian Zhong bronze bell sets? Why are they important in Chinese history? (6:05–6:11)

6. Describe a gamelan orchestra. What does it reflect about Indonesian culture? (7:10–7:26)

7. How did Roman armies use percussion on the battlefield? Why was rhythm useful to them? (8:00–8:09)

8. Which military instrument became part of classical orchestras during the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries? Explain how its role changed. (10:54–11:05)

9. How was the steelpan created in twentieth-century Trinidad? What makes it different from an unpitched drum? (12:44–12:55)

10. How have electronic music and drum machines changed the creation of rhythm? (14:56–15:16)

#### Modern Percussion Notation

11. In modern drum-kit notation, how does the position of a note on the stave tell the performer which instrument to play?

12. What do different noteheads, rests and accents tell a percussionist?

13. How do the time signature and note values help a performer count and reproduce a groove?

14. Why is a notation key useful when reading a drum or percussion part?

15. What can modern notation communicate clearly? What parts of a percussion tradition might still need to be learned by listening, watching or working with another musician?

For Questions 1–10, write at least two complete sentences. For Questions 11–15, explain your answer and use an example where possible.

Save your answers as a Markdown file: `.md`

Strand ii — Percussion Notation Infographic

Create one infographic based on a teacher-approved drum or percussion music sheet.

Your infographic may be:

  • digital
  • drawn on paper

Your infographic must include:

  • a clear title
  • the drum or percussion music sheet
  • a notation key
  • labels or callouts around the sheet
  • short explanations of how the notation helps a performer

Annotate the following when they appear:

  • time signature
  • pulse and metre
  • note values
  • rests
  • repeated patterns
  • instruments or sounds
  • layers and instrumental roles
  • accents and dynamics
  • syncopation
  • fills
  • genre or style features

Do not only name symbols. Explain what they tell the performer and how they help reproduce the rhythm or groove.

Show your chosen music sheet to your teacher before creating the final infographic.
