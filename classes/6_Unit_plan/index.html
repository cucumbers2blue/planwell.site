Description

This first MYP Music unit introduces notation as a system of musical communication. Students find information in their Unit Plan, explore the visible elements of a short piano score, and connect symbols with the sounds and actions they represent. They redraw and annotate the score for Task A. Rhythm work continues through listening, clapping, reading, and rhythm dictation.

Inquiry Questions

Factual

  • What is a steady pulse?
  • What information can we find in a musical score?
  • What do notes, rests, bar lines, and time signatures show?

Conceptual

  • How do musical symbols communicate sound and silence?
  • How does a shared pulse help musicians play together?

Debatable

  • Is written notation more useful than learning music by listening and copying?

Curriculum

Aims

  • Understand the relationship between art and its contexts
  • Develop the skills necessary to create and to perform art
  • Communicate through artistic expression

MYP Subject Group Objectives

  • Objective A: Investigating (Year 1 / Novice)

- i: Investigate a musical context related to the statement of inquiry.

- ii: Describe an artwork or performance from the chosen context.

  • Objective B: Developing

- Practically explore pulse, rhythm, and notation for later music-making.

- Present a simple intention for a rhythm pattern.

  • Objective C: Creating/Performing

- Perform and/or create a short rhythm pattern with a steady pulse.

  • Objective D: Evaluating

- Appraise their own rhythm work.

- Reflect on their development as a musician.

Content

Factual Knowledge

  • Steady pulse and rhythm
  • Sound and silence
  • Staff, clef, and key signature
  • Time signature and bar lines
  • Basic notes and rests
  • Other elementary symbols found in the selected piano score

Conceptual Understanding

  • Musical notation is a system of communication.
  • Symbols represent sounds, silences, and performance instructions.
  • A shared pulse helps musicians coordinate music together.
  • The same written score can guide different musicians.

Procedural Knowledge

  • Open and find information in the Grade 6 Unit Plan.
  • Identify important elements in a short piano score.
  • Redraw a short score with its symbols in the correct places.
  • Annotate symbols with short, accurate explanations.
  • Hear, copy, read, and write short rhythms.

Skills

Subject-Specific Skills

  • Finding pulse and rhythm in a short musical example
  • Reading elementary musical notation
  • Copying notation carefully
  • Explaining what musical symbols communicate
  • Listening to and writing short rhythm patterns

ATL Skills

  • Communication: explain how symbols tell musicians what to play.
  • Self-management: complete a careful score redraw over several lessons.
  • Research: find information in the Unit Plan and in a musical score.
  • Reflection: use peer and self-review to improve the final annotated score.

IB Learner Profile

Attribute: Communicators

Students explore how musicians communicate through pulse, sound, silence, and written symbols.

International Mindedness

Students recognise that musicians may learn by reading, listening, copying, moving, and performing. Written notation is one useful communication system, but it is not the only way people make and share music.

Connections

Language Development

Teach music words through the projected score, teacher modelling, colour, gesture, and repetition. Students may use labels and short sentences. Full paragraphs are not required.

Information Communication Technology

Formative A is completed on paper. Students who can independently open their Google Doc may type instead. Task A is completed by hand so that technology access does not prevent students from showing musical understanding.

Learning Experiences

1. Complete Formative A by opening the Unit Plan and finding five written answers.

2. View a projected piano score and identify any familiar elements.

3. Hear the score and connect visible symbols with musical sound and action.

4. Practise pulse, note values, rests, bar lines, and time signatures through clapping and movement.

5. Study one short teacher-selected piano score for Task A.

6. Redraw the score and annotate its musical symbols and elements.

7. Pause Task A when the fixed midterm preparation period begins.

8. Practise short rhythm dictations for the school midterm exam.

9. Resume Task A after midterms and complete peer and self-review.

Assessment

Summative Tasks

  • Task A (Criterion A): redraw and annotate one short piano score, explaining how its symbols communicate musical information — see Task A: Notation Time.
  • Task BC (Criteria B and C): to be planned after Task A, using evidence of student readiness.
  • Task D (Criterion D): to be planned as an accessible reflection on musical learning.

Formative Assessment

  • Formative A: open the Grade 6 Unit Plan and find five answers.
  • Projected-score diagnostic: identify or make a sensible guess about circled musical elements.
  • Teacher checks during score redrawing and annotation.
  • Short rhythm listening, clapping, reading, and dictation exercises.
  • Midterm rhythm dictation is a school exam and is separate from the Criterion A deadline.

Peer and Self-assessment

After the midterm exams, students check whether a partner can find each labelled element and understand each explanation. Students then improve their own annotated score.

Standardization and Moderation

As the sole music teacher, standardization relies on the Year 1 MYP Arts Criterion A task-specific clarification, the common teacher-selected score, the completed annotated scores, and consistent best-fit judgment across the class.

Feedback

Feedback is immediate and specific. The teacher identifies one symbol, drawing, or explanation to correct, then gives the student time to improve it.

Differentiation

Advanced Students

  • Annotate additional score elements.
  • Explain how two symbols work together.
  • Perform or demonstrate a short section of the score.

Struggling Students

  • Use a larger printed score and trace selected elements before redrawing.
  • Annotate fewer teacher-selected elements.
  • Use the sentence frame and a visual word bank.
  • Explain an element orally before writing.

ELL Support

  • Use an illustrated word bank.
  • Model one complete annotation.
  • Accept labels and short sentences.
  • Pair spoken explanations with pointing and colour.

Resources

  • One short teacher-selected piano score
  • Projector
  • Paper, pencils, erasers, rulers, and coloured pencils
  • Large printed score copies where needed
  • Rhythm cards or short teacher-played rhythm examples

Reflection

Prior to Teaching

Students have very different levels of digital independence and prior notation knowledge. Paper is the default so that account and file-management problems do not block musical learning. Google Docs remains optional for students who can use it independently.

During Teaching

Record which score elements students recognise, which explanations need modelling, and how much rhythm-dictation support is required.

After Teaching

Review the annotated scores and midterm rhythm evidence before planning Task BC and Task D.
