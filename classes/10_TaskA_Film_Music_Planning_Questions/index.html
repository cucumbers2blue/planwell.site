Grade 10 Music - Task A: Film Music Planning Questions

Use these questions to help you make decisions as you plan your investigation. You do not need to answer every question or submit your answers unless your teacher asks you to.

Choosing Your Scene

1. Which scene are you considering, and what makes its music interesting enough to investigate?

2. Does the scene use an original composed score, rather than mainly a licensed song?

3. What changes in the music during the scene? If very little changes, is this the best scene to choose?

4. Can you identify at least three specific moments where the music affects how the scene feels or what the audience expects?

5. Is the scene short enough to analyse closely—about two to five minutes?

6. Can you find reliable information about the film, composer, and score?

7. What difficulties might this scene create for you? Would another scene give you stronger evidence?

Deciding Your Focus

8. What is the main question you want your investigation to answer?

9. Which audience response will you focus on: tension, danger, wonder, sadness, sympathy, excitement, uncertainty, or something else?

10. What does the audience see in the scene, and what does the music make them believe or feel beyond those visuals?

11. Does the score support the visuals, contradict them, or make them more complicated?

12. How would the meaning of the scene change if the music were removed?

13. What different meaning could the same visuals have with completely different music?

Planning Your Analysis

14. Which musical features are most important in this scene: timbre, harmony, melody, rhythm, tempo, dynamics, texture, leitmotif, synchronization, or silence?

15. Which three to five timestamps will provide your strongest evidence?

16. At each chosen timestamp, can you explain the chain: musical choice → audience response → changed meaning?

17. Are you explaining how the music works, or only describing what you hear and see?

18. Which screenshot, clip, timeline entry, or score excerpt will make each claim easier for your audience to understand?

19. What film-music history or genre convention genuinely helps explain your scene? What background information can you leave out?

Building Your Presentation

20. What should appear visually on the slides, and what fuller explanation belongs in your presenter notes?

21. What is the single most important claim you want your audience to remember?

22. Do your sources serve different purposes—film-music history, film context, composer or score, and the scene itself?

23. Which idea from this investigation could you later use in your own music for a visual scene?

24. If your teacher challenged your interpretation, which piece of evidence would best defend it?

25. What decision do you need to make next: choose a scene, narrow your question, research, create a timeline, or start building the presentation?
