Grade 6 Design course overview

Course context

  • Grade 6 Design is taught separately to 6AD and 6BD.
  • Each class has four periods per week, giving enough time for explicit teaching, guided practice, retrieval practice and frequent formative assessment.
  • One class began the course ahead of the other because the classes did not have consistent teacher coverage. Both classes will restart from the beginning and follow the same weekly sequence so they become aligned.
  • The active Term 1 unit is Designing for a Digital World, running for 18 weeks from 15 July to 1 December 2026.
  • Students are working at MYP Year 1 / novice level.
  • The unit's statement of inquiry is: Understanding how digital systems work helps individuals design ethical, responsible solutions that protect their identity and relationships online.

Term 1 curriculum

The current intended sequence is:

| Weeks | Focus |

| --- | --- |

| 1–4 | Digital Citizenship and Online Safety |

| 5–9 | Computer Systems |

| 10–13 | Design Foundations and File Management |

| 14–18 | Introduction to Coding and the Design Cycle mini-project |

The main knowledge areas are:

  • Digital citizenship, online safety, privacy and personal information.
  • Digital footprints and online reputation.
  • Strong passwords and account safety.
  • Netiquette and respectful online communication.
  • Hardware and software.
  • Input, output and storage devices.
  • Basic design principles.
  • Files, folders, naming conventions and file formats.
  • Algorithms, sequencing and introductory block-based coding.
  • The MYP Design Cycle: inquire, develop, create and evaluate.

Pre-mid-term knowledge phase

Students will sit a mid-term examination in September. The examination will be delivered as a Google Forms questionnaire.

Before the examination, the priority is knowledge acquisition and consolidation. Students will:

  • Receive direct teaching and examples for each new topic.
  • Write important definitions, examples and explanations in physical notebooks.
  • Revise from their notes.
  • Use interactive flashcard games for retrieval practice.
  • Complete short Google Forms checks so misconceptions can be identified early.

The exact mid-term scope still needs to be confirmed. The current unit sequence suggests that it may include both Digital Citizenship and the Computer Systems content taught before the examination.

Weekly learning rhythm

| Period | Main purpose |

| --- | --- |

| 1 | Introduce and explain the week's new concepts; students build notebook notes. |

| 2 | Revision, flashcards and correction of misunderstandings. |

| 3 | Further revision, flashcards and application through examples or scenarios. |

| 4 | Low-stakes formative task in Google Forms. |

The Period 4 Google Form is formative, not summative. Its results should inform the next week's teaching and identify students who need extra explanation or practice.

Codex will evaluate each student's weekly response and write a short, constructive comment in the student's personal Markdown record. This feedback should be concise, kind and actionable. It should identify one demonstrated strength or secure idea and the most important next step.

Flashcard practice may later include a timer or competitive element. Any competition should support repeated practice without discouraging students who need more time.

Current student resources

Two public flashcard games have been created:

  • Hardware and Software
  • Digital Citizenship

The Digital Citizenship game currently covers:

  • Digital footprints.
  • Password safety and private information.
  • Netiquette.

Cards answered incorrectly return later until the student answers them correctly. The game intentionally excludes the notebook section titled Peripheral parts attached to computer.

Post-mid-term project phase

Formal summative assessment will begin only after the September mid-term examination. Summative work will be project-based and will use the MYP Design Cycle.

The active unit plan anticipates a digital product addressing a real digital-world need. Across the project, students should generate evidence for:

  • Criterion A — Inquiring and analysing: identify a problem or need and conduct relevant research.
  • Criterion B — Developing ideas: define success criteria, develop ideas and communicate a chosen plan.
  • Criterion C — Creating the solution: plan and create a functioning digital product while documenting changes.
  • Criterion D — Evaluating: test the product, evaluate it against the success criteria and suggest improvements.

The exact project brief, product format and division of criteria across summative tasks are not yet decided.

Teaching principles emerging from the plan

  • Retrieval practice should be frequent, but flashcards should not become the entire learning experience.
  • Students also need to explain choices, apply knowledge to scenarios and correct misconceptions.
  • Notebook writing provides a durable personal revision source and should emphasize meaning rather than copying large amounts of text.
  • Weekly assessment should change subsequent teaching—for example, by triggering a short reteach, targeted revision group or additional practice.
  • Both 6AD and 6BD should follow the same weekly content and assessment sequence. The priority is to keep the classes aligned, even when one class could move ahead more quickly.
  • The pre-mid-term knowledge phase and post-mid-term project phase should remain clearly distinguished for students.
